Log in with it once

On your computer, run:

A key is a pair of files on your computer that lets you log in without typing a password. Unlike a password, it can't be guessed, so once it works Roostack can turn passwords off.

  1. Make a key on your computer. Skip this if you already have one.
    {{segmented "os" "Your computer" "windows" (choice "windows" "Windows") (choice "mac" "macOS") (choice "linux" "Linux") "size=small"}}

    Open PowerShell (from the Start menu) and run:

    ssh-keygen -t ed25519

    Press Enter to keep the file where it suggests, and choose a passphrase, which protects the key if someone gets your computer. Then show the public key:

    type $env:USERPROFILE\.ssh\id_ed25519.pub

    Open Terminal (in Applications, Utilities) and run:

    ssh-keygen -t ed25519

    Press Enter to keep the file where it suggests, and choose a passphrase, which protects the key if someone gets your Mac. Then copy the public key:

    pbcopy < ~/.ssh/id_ed25519.pub

    Open a terminal and run:

    ssh-keygen -t ed25519

    Press Enter to keep the file where it suggests, and choose a passphrase, which protects the key if someone gets your computer. Then show the public key:

    cat ~/.ssh/id_ed25519.pub

How Roostack adds the key

roostack-agent checks the key (one public key, no options) and that the account exists and is in the sudo group, then in a short systemd job of its own (roostack-ssh-key), since its sandbox can't read home folders, adds the key to ~/.ssh/authorized_keys (the folder 700, the file 600, both owned by the account, a link in their place refused). It never adds a key to root, and never makes an account or sets a password: anything running as Roostack could then give itself root. Adding a key is in History, with the key's fingerprint.
